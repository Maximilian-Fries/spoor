Why Luftlage exists

When a drone closes an airport, nobody can say where it came from.

Drones are shutting runways, dropping contraband into prisons and crossing military bases. The sensors to detect them exist. What does not exist is an answer to the question that decides the response: who flew it, and from where.

Without attribution every incident ends the same way. The drone leaves, the pilot is never found, and nobody can say whether it was a hobbyist or a hostile act. In our research we found no product that turns sensor tracks into evidence of a launch point. That is the gap Luftlage closes.

After 144 suspected drone sightings across Europe since 2024, investigators could not tie a single drone to a launch site with signals, wreckage or video. Sweden's military representative to NATO calls attribution often almost impossible.IISS report and critique, July 2026
What Luftlage delivers

A track, a launch area, and proof.

One site sees a dot for a few seconds. A network of sites sees a path, and a path has a beginning.

Output 1

A track across sites

Nodes hosted by many sites hand a drone from one to the next, so a track does not end at a fence. The network records every track it sees.

Output 2

A launch area

Replayed backwards, a track leads to where the drone entered coverage. The heading beyond that point gives an estimated launch area, on a road or off the coast.

Output 3

Evidence that holds up

Cued by the network, a police drone follows the intruder until it lands and films where it goes. A reusable drone has to return to someone.

01 / 04

Sites host nodes

A node is a small box with microphones, a camera and passive radio receivers. Companies, operators and public buildings mount one and get local alerts. Classification runs on the device, and no audio leaves it.

02 / 04

The network tracks

As a drone crosses coverage, nodes hand its track from one to the next and fuse their bearings into one position. A single site sees a dot. The network sees a path.

03 / 04

The track is traced back

Every track is recorded. Replayed backwards it leads to the point where the drone entered coverage, and the heading beyond gives an estimated launch area, with the uncertainty stated.

04 / 04

A police drone follows it home

Luftlage cues drones that police already operate. The chase drone follows the intruder until it lands and films where it goes. That recording is the proof.

Why it compounds

Every new site makes every trace longer.

A single site sees a drone for seconds. Neighbouring nodes see it before it arrives, tracks across sites lead back to a launch point, and each site adds data that cuts false alarms.

Coverage grows cluster by cluster: an industrial park, a port, a defense-industry region. The state pays for the combined picture. The hosts make it exist.
